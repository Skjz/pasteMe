### Added

- A settings window, opened with 设置… (⌘,) in the menu bar menu. A sidebar on the left lists its pages and each page groups its switches like System Settings: 通用 (launch at login, the copy sound, whether the accessibility permission is on with a button to System Settings, and the three global shortcuts), 面板 (cursor style, picked from two live previews; showing the panel during screen sharing; pasting images as files), 链接预览 (the three levels, each with what it requests written underneath; a level takes effect when chosen instead of asking in a dialog), 历史 (automatic clearing and clearing the history, both still asking before they delete entries), 应用别名 and 关于 (version and project links). The window has a fixed size of 620 × 690 points and can't be resized. It is modeled on Alcove's settings window: the whole window shows the desktop through one translucent material, the sidebar and the pages are separated by a thin line, and settings sit in softly tinted groups. 应用别名 lists each app's icon, name and the names it can already be found by, with all alias fields lined up and a search field at the top; 为“…”设置别名… in a card's right-click menu opens this page, scrolled to that app with the cursor in its alias field.
- Pinboards: named, colored groups for entries you want to keep. Tabs at the top left of the panel switch between 历史 (the history) and each Pinboard; click a tab, press ⌘← / ⌘→, or press ⇧⌘V again while the panel is open to move to the next one (with no Pinboards, ⇧⌘V still closes the panel). Pin the selected card with ⌘P (to the Pinboard you used last), from 固定到 in its right-click menu (tick or untick any Pinboard, or create a new one), or by dragging the card onto a tab. Inside a Pinboard, cards keep the order you give them: drag a card over another to reorder. ⌘⌫ there takes the entry out of that Pinboard only (it stays in the history), and ⌘Z puts it back in its place. Create a Pinboard with the + button, rename it by double-clicking its tab, and change its color or delete it from the tab's right-click menu; deleting a Pinboard asks first and keeps its entries in the history. Pinned cards show a small pin in the history, and clearing the history now keeps pinned entries, in their order, and says how many it keeps. Copying a pinned entry again moves it to the top of the history but not within its Pinboards. The panel opens on the history tab each time. (#9)
- Filter by Pinboard: a Pinboard menu next to the time filter (pick one or more), or type `@` and choose a Pinboard from the list. Search, type, app and time filters also work inside a Pinboard tab. ⌘J, or 在历史里查看 in the right-click menu, clears the search and filters, switches to the history, and selects the entry among what you copied just before and after it, even if it is older than the 200 entries the panel usually shows. (#9)
- Press space on a card to preview its full content in a window above the panel: all of a long text (with its formatting), an image at full size that you can zoom (pinch, the zoom buttons, 适合窗口 and 实际大小), a link with its page title and preview picture plus 在浏览器中打开, and files through Quick Look. Space or esc closes it, ← → switch to the neighboring cards while it stays open, and ↩ pastes. Image previews list the text recognized in the image with a 拷贝文字 button. (#10)
- Edit text and links: click into the preview's text, or press ⌘E (or choose 编辑… from the right-click menu), and change it; the change is saved when the preview closes and is used for search and paste from then on. Smart quotes, dash substitution and autocorrect are off, so code stays as typed. If the edited text matches another entry, the two become one entry that keeps the newer copy time, its title, and both entries' Pinboards. Editing a link's address clears its old page preview. (#10)
- Give any entry a title from the preview's title field, or right in the card header (see below). Cards show the title in their header, and the type (with its text subtype) moves in front of the copy time on the second line, for example 文本 · hex · 4 分钟前, so a titled card still shows what it holds. Search finds entries by title. ⌘N writes a new text entry in the preview; it goes to the top of the history when the preview closes, and into the current Pinboard when one is open. (#10)
- Link previews, off by default, with two levels on the 链接预览 page of the settings window. 只取网站图标 opens only the site's home page and shows its icon on the card; the copied address itself is never opened, so no link can be used up. 完整预览 opens the copied address and shows the page title, preview picture and site icon, searchable by title; links that look single-use (words like token, reset, verify, login or unsubscribe in the address, or a long random string such as a token, signature or UUID) still get only the site icon, and so does a short link that redirects to such an address. Each level says what it requests right under it, including that a few single-use links can't be recognized. Pages are read as plain HTML up to `</head>` (at most 2 MB): no scripts run, no cookies are sent or kept, nothing else on the page is loaded, and links to files such as installers aren't downloaded; a link to an image uses the image itself. Addresses on this Mac or the local network (localhost, `.local`, private and Tailscale IP ranges, single-word host names) are never requested, redirects to them aren't followed, links copied earlier are not fetched in bulk, and nothing is requested while it is off. Reddit, whose pages show nothing without a browser and block some networks outright, gets post titles from its oEmbed API (subreddit and share links show the subreddit name, such as r/swift) and its icon from a fixed address. Tracking parameters added when sharing (`utm_…`, `si`, `share_id`, `fbclid` and the like) are dropped before judging and requesting, so YouTube and Reddit share links get full previews. Link cards that have a preview use a poster layout: centered, with the page title in a serif face (New York, with Songti for Chinese) and the site name in spaced capitals between two thin rules; a preview picture sits on top, or else the site icon, enlarged, with a soft glow in the icon's own color (icons with transparent corners sit on a white tile so they stay visible in dark mode). Without a page title, the title is read from the address, for example "swiftlang / swift" for a GitHub repository or "Swift" for a Wikipedia article. Icons are kept at up to 128 pixels, and PasteMe also tries a site's `fluid-icon` and the conventional `/apple-touch-icon.png` to get a sharp one. A link that fails or times out keeps its plain card and is not retried; sites that need a browser to show anything (Zhihu, for example) stay plain. (#11)
- Text subtypes: text that is, as a whole, a color (`#RGB`, `#RRGGBB` with or without alpha, CSS `rgb()`/`rgba()`/`hsl()`/`hsla()` in comma or space syntax), a Unix timestamp (10-digit seconds, optionally with a fraction, or 13-digit milliseconds, between 2000 and 2100), `hexdump -C` or `xxd` output (also dumps in the same layout with any number of bytes per line and a character column without the `|` bars, as packet capture tools copy them), hex (prefixed with `0x` or `\x`, bytes separated by spaces or colons, or a continuous run with both digits and letters, such as a Git commit hash) or Base64 (standard or URL-safe, with or without padding) is labeled with that subtype in the card header, for example "文本 · Base64". Color cards show a swatch, the value and its other notation; hexdump, hex and Base64 cards use a monospaced font. Type `@` and pick 颜色, 时间戳, hexdump, hex, Base64 or 代码 (or write `type:base64`) to see only that subtype; the 文本 filter still includes all of them. Ordinary words, identifiers, paths, phone numbers, order numbers and UUIDs are not mistaken for hex or Base64, a value inside a longer paragraph is not labeled, and content over 1 MB is not examined. Text copied before this version is labeled in small batches shortly after launch. The history database moves to version 7 (one added column). (#26)
- Conversions: timestamp cards show the matching local time, time zone and relative time, for example `2025-10-17 16:00:00 GMT+8`. A 转换 submenu in the right-click menu of text and link cards, also opened with ⌘T on the selected card, offers: Base64 encoding for any text; decoding for Base64 (or hex and hexdump when the bytes aren't text); text (decoded as UTF-8), hexdump or hex, and a C array in the same format as `xxd -i` for hex and hexdump; local time and ISO 8601 (UTC) for timestamps. Conversions that can't be done are listed with the reason, for example bytes that aren't valid UTF-8. The result is recorded as a new entry at the top of the history and selected, so ↩ pastes it; the original stays as it was, the result has no source app (like text written with ⌘N), and a result that matches an existing entry moves that entry to the top. Converting inside a Pinboard or with filters on switches to the unfiltered history so the result is visible, and a decoded web address becomes a link. Content over 1 MB isn't offered for conversion. (#27)
- Code highlighting: code in Swift, Objective-C, C, C++, C#, Java, Kotlin, Go, Rust, JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Ruby, PHP, Shell, SQL, HTML, XML, CSS, JSON or YAML is recognized when it is copied, and its card shows the language in the header (for example "文本 · Swift") and the code in a monospaced font, colored by keywords, strings, numbers, comments, type names, function calls, attributes and keys, in the colors of Xcode's default light and dark themes; the colors follow the system appearance. Indentation is kept (indentation shared by all lines is removed and tabs show as four spaces), and long lines are cut at the right edge with "…" instead of wrapping. When in doubt PasteMe treats the text as plain text: paragraphs, Markdown, emails, chat logs and math are not shown as code. Text copied from code editors (Xcode, VS Code, Cursor, Zed, JetBrains IDEs, Sublime Text and others) and terminals needs less evidence, and a single command copied from a terminal counts as Shell. Pasting is unchanged: the original text is pasted. (#28)
- Title a card without opening the preview: press ⌘R on the selected card, or choose 添加标题 (修改标题 once it has one) from its right-click menu, and the card's header turns into a text field with the current title selected. ↩ saves, esc cancels, and clicking elsewhere, switching tabs or closing the panel also saves; an empty title removes it, so the header shows the type again. While you type, space, the arrow keys and other card shortcuts go to the field. (#33)
- Clear old history automatically: on the 历史 page of the settings window, set 自动清除历史 to 7 天前的, 30 天前的, 90 天前的 or 1 年前的 (the default is 永不). Entries in a Pinboard are never cleared, and the time counts from the last time an entry was copied or pasted, so entries you keep using stay. PasteMe checks at launch and then every hour, skipping the check while the panel is open. Choosing a period that would delete entries right away first says how many and asks to confirm. (#34)
- Hear a short sound each time PasteMe records a copy: turn on 复制时播放提示音 on the 通用 page of the settings window (off by default; turning it on plays the sound once). Copying something already in the history counts too, since it moves to the top. It stays silent while recording is paused, for passwords and other content PasteMe doesn't keep, and for what PasteMe itself puts on the clipboard (pasting from the panel, ⌘C on a card, Paste Stack). The sound is PasteMe's own: a sheet of paper sliding into a clipboard's clip and the clip snapping shut, short and quieter than the system's alert sounds. Four slightly different takes play in turn, the way real paper never sounds quite the same twice. It is silent when 播放用户界面音效 (Play user interface sound effects) is off in System Settings. Because the clipboard is checked every half second, the sound can come up to half a second after ⌘C. (#25)
- Press ⌘C on the selected card (or choose 拷贝 from its right-click menu) to put it on the clipboard without pasting, for pasting later yourself. The panel stays open and the cards stay where they are; a short notice confirms the copy, and the entry moves to the top of the history once the panel closes. In the search field ⌘C still copies the selected text. (#35)
- Text pasted into Finder from the panel becomes a .txt file in the current folder, named after the entry's title, or else its first line (up to 40 characters), or else the copy time. Pasting into other apps still gives plain text, and dragging a text card into Finder also gives a .txt file instead of a text clipping, while dragging it into other apps gives the text. The file is UTF-8 without a BOM. (#36)
- MD5 and SHA-256: the 转换 menu of text and link cards (right-click or ⌘T) lists MD5 and SHA-256 of the text as UTF-8 bytes (Base64 and hex text is not decoded first), and file cards have 计算 MD5 and 计算 SHA-256 in their right-click menu, disabled with the reason when a file is missing or is a folder. The result, lowercase hex, is recorded as a new entry at the top of the history and selected, like a conversion; several files give one `<hash>  <path>` line each, as `shasum` does. Hashes are computed in the background, files in 1 MB chunks, so large files don't block the panel, and text over 1 MB can be hashed too. (#37)
- Drag cards out of the panel into other apps: text and links as text, files as the files themselves (files that have since been moved are left out, with a notice if none are left), and images as both the picture and a PNG file, so they also work in Finder, browser upload fields and chat windows that only take files. The panel closes as soon as the drag leaves it, so it doesn't cover the drop target. (#12)
- PasteMe has its own app icon instead of the generic one: a white clipboard with three history entries on an orange background. On macOS 26 and later it is a Liquid Glass icon that follows the icon style chosen in System Settings (default, dark, clear or tinted); macOS 14 and 15 show a flat version of the same design. It shows in Finder, Spotlight, System Settings and PasteMe's own dialogs. The menu bar icon is unchanged.
- 以文件粘贴 in an image card's right-click menu pastes the image together with a PNG file, which Finder turns into a file; turn on 图片以文件粘贴 on the 面板 page of the settings window to do this for every image. The files are named after the copy time, for example `PasteMe 2026-10-16 14.41.55.png`, live in `~/Library/Caches/PasteMe/Files`, and are deleted after a day without use, checked at launch and every hour. (#12)

- Type `@` in the search field, or anywhere on the card list, to pick a filter from a list: the four types and the apps in your history. Keep typing to narrow it down by a type's Chinese or English name, or by any name or alias of an app (`@图`, `@finder` for 访达, `@wx` once you have given WeChat that alias); apps whose name starts with the text come first. ↑ ↓ move through the list, ↩ or Tab picks the highlighted entry, and a click picks any entry. The choice becomes a filter shown on the filter buttons, and the `@` word leaves the search field. While the list is open, the half-written word doesn't narrow the results. esc closes the list and searches for the word as plain text instead, for example `@gmail.com`; an `@` in the middle of a word, as in an email address, never opens the list. `type:` and `app:` keep working.
- Deleting a card (⌘⌫, or 删除 in its right-click menu) breaks it into fine dust that sweeps from left to right, drifts upward and fades out over about 1.4 seconds. The other cards stay where they are until the dust is gone, then the cards to the right slide in, settling without bumping into the card on their left. Deleting several cards in a row dissolves each one in its own place, and the cards to the right wait for all of them and then slide over in one go instead of one slot at a time. ⌘Z plays it backwards: the dust drifts back and reassembles the card, once no other card is in the way (the cards to its right make room first if they had already moved in). Undoing before the dust is gone reverses it from where it is, and deleting a card again while it reassembles dissolves it from where it is. No card slides under dust that is still in the air: dust starts exactly where the card is on screen even while cards are sliding, follows a card that is pushed aside while it reassembles, and reassembles at the size the card will have when it shows. The dust is drawn on the GPU, so opening, closing and scrolling the panel stay as smooth as before. With Reduce Motion turned on, the card just disappears and the others slide in.
- Search finds entries by any name of the app they were copied from: Finder entries (recorded as 访达 on a Chinese system) also turn up for "finder", WeChat entries for "wechat", Terminal entries for "terminal", and the same works after `app:`. The names come from each app's own localized names in English and your system languages, so nothing has to be set up. You can add your own aliases too, for example "wx" for WeChat: on the 应用别名 page of the settings window, or right-click a card and choose "为“微信”设置别名…" to jump straight to that app. The page lists every app in your history with the names it can already be found by; separate several aliases with commas. Aliases apply to the next search right away and are kept across restarts.

- Paste Stack: press ⌃⇧⌘C (or choose "Paste Stack" in the menu bar menu) and copy several things in a row; then each ⌘V in another app pastes the next one in the order you copied them, until the stack is empty. A small window in the top-right corner lists what is waiting and marks the next entry; hover a row to take it out of the stack. esc, ⌃⇧⌘C or the window's close button leaves Paste Stack early, after which ⌘V behaves normally again. Copies still go into the history as usual, and pasting from the stack doesn't add duplicates. The menu bar icon changes while Paste Stack is on. It needs the accessibility permission (PasteMe asks for it if missing) and refuses to start while recording is paused. Pasting from the panel while a stack is active doesn't take an entry from the stack. (#6)
- Text inside copied images and image files is recognized on this Mac (Vision, Simplified and Traditional Chinese and English) a moment after you copy them, so screenshots can be found by searching for words in them. Image cards with recognized text show a small text icon; when a search matches the text in an image, the bottom of the card shows the matching words. Images copied before this version are recognized in the background after launch, one at a time with a short pause in between. Recognition never runs on the main thread, and an image without text is checked only once. (#5)
- Search filters next to the panel's search field: four type buttons (文本, 链接, 图片, 文件) that can be combined, a menu of the apps that appear in your history with their icons (most used first), and a time menu (今天, 昨天, 最近 7 天, 最近 30 天). Filters combine with each other and with the search text. You can also type them: `type:图片` or `type:image` (the first letters are enough, e.g. `type:im`), and `app:微信` for apps whose name contains the text; a full-width colon works too. When nothing matches, the panel lists the conditions that ruled everything out. esc clears the search text and filters together, and they reset each time the panel opens. A database index on the source app keeps filtering fast; with 10,000 entries a filtered search takes well under 50 ms. (#4)
- ⌃⇧⌘V pauses recording until you press it again, from any app. The "暂停记录" menu item is now a submenu that also pauses for 5 minutes, 30 minutes or 1 hour and resumes on its own when the time is up. While recording is paused the menu bar icon has a slash through it, the menu item turns into "恢复记录" (with the time it resumes on its own), and a short notice appears on screen whenever recording pauses or resumes. Pausing is not remembered across restarts, so PasteMe always starts out recording. ⌥⇧⌘V was avoided because many apps use it for Paste and Match Style. (#7)

Turn on 共享屏幕时显示面板 on the 面板 page of the settings window to let it be captured, for example when recording a tutorial. The setting is kept across restarts. ### Fixed

- On macOS 26 and later, PasteMe uses the system's current design: window buttons, switches, buttons, menus and alerts look like those of other apps. The app built by `scripts/bundle.sh` declared the macOS 14 SDK, so macOS ran it with the old look; the script now records the SDK it was built with and stops if that fails.
- The panel opens at the newest card even when you had scrolled the card list with the trackpad last time without changing the selection; before, it opened where you had left it, with the newest card off screen. (#39)
- App icons in card headers, and site icons on link cards, are no longer blurry on a Retina screen when they were first drawn while only a non-Retina display was connected. The icon images are now always drawn at least at 2x. (#19)
- Screenshots copied from CleanShot X are recorded as images, not as files. CleanShot puts the screenshot on the clipboard both as picture data and as the PNG file it saved, so PasteMe kept only the file. Now, when the clipboard holds a single file and its picture data is exactly that file, PasteMe records the picture, which gets a thumbnail and text recognition like any other image. Copying an image file in Finder still records the file. Screenshots copied before this change stay file entries.
- Return, the arrow keys and esc pressed while an input method is composing text in the search field (for example Pinyin before a candidate is chosen) go to the input method instead of pasting the selected card, moving the selection or clearing the search.
- Opening and closing the panel no longer stutters. After the card color and cursor changes, about a third of the frames while the panel slid up missed the 120 Hz deadline: every card clipped its enlarged header icon at run time, so each card had to be recomposited on every frame of its entrance animation. The cropped icon with its shadow is now drawn once per app as an image that fits the header. Closing also no longer blocks for 30–50 ms moving the panel's content into a separate window for the slide-out; the panel slides out itself.

- Pressing ⌘⌫ in the panel's search field to delete the typed text no longer deletes the selected clipboard entry. (#1)

The benchmark's generated history now gives its links previews (site icons, half with page titles), and two of the first cards are links, so link cards are measured too. `scripts/bench-panel.sh` can run against a copy of a real history (`PASTEME_BENCH_HISTORY`) and with release optimizations (`PASTEME_BENCH_RELEASE=1`), for stutter that only shows up with a user's own data. ADR 0007 records why Pinboards point at history entries instead of keeping their own copies, and the architecture guide covers Pinboards, the preview and its focus rules, dragging and link previews.

- The panel opens with keyboard focus on the card list instead of the search field, so ← →, ⌘⌫ and ⌘Z act on the cards right away; typing still goes straight into the search field. esc, after clearing the search and filters, also puts focus back on the card list. The search field's placeholder mentions `@`, and the hint line on the card list says that typing searches.
- New `FilterMention` and `ClipKind.matching(_:)` in `PasteMeCore` read the `@` word being typed and the types it matches, and `AppNames.apps(matching:)` lists matching apps with prefix matches first. `PanelKeymap` takes whether the suggestion list is showing and whether an input method is composing. Debug builds given `PASTEME_SNAPSHOT_QUERY` now move focus to the search field before typing, as typing does, so `PASTEME_SNAPSHOT_QUERY=@` renders the suggestion list.
- Text fields support the usual editing shortcuts (⌘X, ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘A, ⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z), and ⌘W closes the settings window. PasteMe has no menu bar of its own, so these come from a hidden main menu.
- Tests for the delete and undo animations in three layers. `PanelFlushTests` checks what the view sees halfway through a delete or undo (SwiftUI renders pending changes before `withAnimation`). `DeleteAnimationScenarioTests` runs the real model and dust effect on a virtual clock against a simulated screen and checks every frame that dust and cards line up, cards don't overlap each other or slide under dust, and gaps close only after all dust is done, in named scenarios and in random sequences of deletes, undos and waits (60 seeds by default, more with `PASTEME_FUZZ_SEEDS`; `PM_TRACE=<seed>` prints a seed frame by frame). `SwiftUIAssumptionTests` hosts the real `HistoryView` in an offscreen window to confirm the SwiftUI behaviors the other two rely on. They found and fixed several cases that manual testing hadn't hit, such as two cards swapping places when a card was restored and another deleted in the same moment.
- `scripts/bench-panel.sh --delete` measures the delete and undo animations the same way as opening and closing the panel, and debug builds take `PASTEME_SNAPSHOT_DELETE=1` to delete the selected card just before rendering the panel. `docs/agents/ui-performance.md` adds reference values for both and notes that playing video during a measurement makes the numbers meaningless.
- New `AppNames` in `PasteMeCore` finds source apps by their recorded name, the localized names their bundles declare (`AppBundleNames`) and the user's aliases (`Settings.appAliases`); `ClipQuery.resolvingAppNames(_:)` adds the matching apps to a search. App names are read in the background a few seconds after launch so the first search doesn't stall.
- `HistoryModel` plays delete and undo animations through the `CardRemovalEffect` protocol and keeps a gap where a deleted card was (`HistoryModel.cards`) until every delete and undo animation has ended, while the list itself (`items`), the selection and ⌘1–⌘9 already reflect the delete. It asks the effect for the slide animation whenever cards move (`slideAnimation()`), and `CardDust` treats the layout changes SwiftUI reports right after that as springs from the old to the new position, so it knows where a sliding card is on screen, and how large a card that is being selected or deselected is, from the same spring formulas SwiftUI uses (`SpringCurve`). Gaps are placed by the deleted entry's copy time rather than by slot number. Model changes go through `HistoryModel.animate`, and the dust effect takes an injectable clock and scheduler, so tests can drive both.
- The architecture guide explains how to record the panel for animation bugs (turn on "共享屏幕时显示面板" first) and corrects an earlier claim: a test instance started with a throwaway home still reads and writes the real preferences, because `CFFIXED_USER_HOME` doesn't apply to them.
- New `PasteStack` in `PasteMeCore` keeps the order of a Paste Stack; `Recorder` adds each recorded entry to it while it is on. `Paster.copyToPasteboard(_:as:)` writes an entry to the clipboard and moves it to the top of the history without sending ⌘V. The ⌘V that PasteMe sends itself is marked so the Paste Stack listener ignores it. `ClipQuery` can be limited to a list of ids. (#6)
- Debug builds take `PASTEME_SNAPSHOT_QUERY` alongside `PASTEME_SNAPSHOT` to type a search before rendering the panel, for checking filters and search results without screen recording permission.
- New `EnrichmentQueue` in `PasteMeCore` adds information to entries after they are saved: it reads the image on the main thread, recognizes text in the background through the `TextRecognizing` adapter (`VisionTextRecognizer` in the app, a fake in tests), and writes the result back on the main thread. `Recorder` hands it every copied image. The database moves to `user_version = 3`, which adds an `ocr_text` column (NULL until recognized, empty when an image has no text); search also matches it, and undoing a delete keeps it. (#5)
- `ClipQuery` gains type, source app (by bundle id or by part of the name), and time conditions, and `ClipQuery.parse(_:)` turns `type:` and `app:` words typed in the search field into those conditions. `ClipStore.sourceApps()` lists the apps in the history by entry count. The database moves to `user_version = 2`, which adds an index on `source_bundle_id`; existing histories are migrated on launch. (#4)
- Deciding whether to record a copy, and pausing, moved from `ClipboardMonitor` into `Recorder` in `PasteMeCore`, covered by `RecorderTests`. `HotKey` is replaced by `HotKeyCenter`, which registers any number of global shortcuts behind one Carbon event handler and runs the one whose id was pressed; before, a second shortcut would have triggered every registered action. (#7)
- The card row has 30 pt margins on both sides and 18 pt between cards. When the selection scrolls the list, the selected card keeps the margin instead of sitting against the panel edge.

- The panel now tracks keyboard focus: the search field when the panel opens, the card list after ↓ or a click on a card. ⌘⌫ and ⌘Z act on the search text while the search field has focus, and delete the selected entry / undo the delete while the card list has focus. ← → move the text cursor when the search field has text, and select cards otherwise. ↑ or typing returns focus to the search field. The selected card's border is brighter when the card list has focus, and the hint line lists the keys for the current focus. (#1)
- `ClipStore.delete(id:)` now returns the deleted entry as a `DeletedClip`, and the new `ClipStore.restore(_:)` puts it back with its original id, time, source and thumbnail. If the same content was copied again in the meantime, the newer entry is kept. Panel key handling moved into `PanelKeymap`, a pure function covered by `PanelKeymapTests`.

- The app code moved out of the `PasteMe` executable target into a new `PasteMeApp` library target, so panel logic can be unit-tested; `PasteMe` now contains only `main.swift`. A new `PasteMeAppTests` target covers moving the selection and which card is selected after a delete.
- `ClipStore.recent(limit:matching:)` is replaced by `ClipStore.items(matching:)`, which takes a `ClipQuery` value. `ClipQuery` currently holds the search text and the result limit; how a query becomes SQL is kept in `ClipQuery.swift`.
- The paste flow moved from `AppDelegate` into `Paster` in `PasteMeCore`. `paste(_:as:into:)` takes `.original` or `.plainText` and a target app, and returns `.pasted` or `.copiedOnly` (no accessibility permission). Writing the pasteboard and sending ⌘V are injected adapters, so tests run against in-memory fakes. The app hides the panel before calling it.
- New `Settings` type in `PasteMeCore`, stored in the `io.github.skjz.PasteMe` defaults domain, where every user-facing switch is defined.
